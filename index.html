Buried by Vesuvius · Revealed by AI

You're looking at a book from 79 AD.

When Mount Vesuvius erupted, it buried a library of papyrus scrolls and baked them into charcoal. They're too fragile to unroll. Now X-rays and AI can read the ink hidden inside, and you can explore 30 pieces from 10 of those scrolls right here.

Start exploring → What am I looking at?

The story in three steps

I

Buried in 79 AD

Vesuvius erupted and buried the town of Herculaneum, including a grand villa with a library. The heat turned its scrolls into lumps of charcoal, but also kept them safe underground for 1,700 years.

II

Too fragile to open

Found in the 1750s, most scrolls crumble if you try to unroll them. So scientists scan them with powerful X-rays and unroll them virtually, one layer at a time.

III

AI finds the ink

The ink is soot on burnt papyrus, almost invisible. An AI trained on known letters spots it. Some of the pictures here were made on an ordinary laptop; others are the team's own published maps, used with credit.

What does the highlight mean?

The highlight shows where the AI thinks there's ink. It's gold to start with, and on any piece you can switch it to black (like ink on paper) or red. Stronger colour means more confident. It's an excellent guess, but not a final reading: some bright marks are cracks or fibres. Turning marks into words is careful work done by experts in ancient writing.

What you can do here

Zoom all the way in

Every piece at a high resolution, down to single strokes, with nothing to install and no account. Works with pinch-zoom on your phone.

See the ink on the papyrus

The gold highlight sits right on top of the real X-ray scan, so you can see where the ink lies on the burnt sheet. Sliders help tired eyes.

Share the exact spot

Found something intriguing? “Share this view” sends a friend to the very place and zoom you're looking at.

And if you have a Windows PC, the free one-click app lets you read other pieces yourself, no graphics card needed.

This only exists because other people shared their work: Domenico Russo made the Reader V3 ink AI; the Vesuvius Challenge team and volunteers scan, trace and openly publish the scrolls and wrote the reading code; community members like Ahggggg showed where Windows users get stuck; and many others trained and published the ink maps you can browse here. I built on top of all of that and hope it adds a small step forward.
