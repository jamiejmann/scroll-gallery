The story of this piece

PHerc. 343P is a related fragment, not the main announced strip. It is in the public data with a published ink map, and some of its marks are bold. There is no separate announcement for this fragment, so treat it as a close-up puzzle, not a new find.

What to look for

Look at the stronger isolated marks. Do they feel like the same kind of strokes as the main PHerc. 343 strip?

What does the gold mean?

The ink on these scrolls is made of soot, just like the burnt papyrus, so it's nearly invisible in X-rays. An AI that learned from known letters paints gold wherever it thinks ink is. Brighter gold means it's more confident. It's a very good guess, not a reading.

For the curious: how this picture was made

This is one segment: a stretch of papyrus that the Vesuvius Challenge team traced through a 3D X-ray scan and flattened into a picture. The ink map is Reader V3, by Domenico Russo, published by the team in the open data and used here with credit. We did not re-run the model. The scan view is the X-ray surface (the middle layers), sometimes shown at a slightly lower resolution so the site stays quick to load.

Size: 2,060 × 3,440 pixels (about 18 × 30 mm).
