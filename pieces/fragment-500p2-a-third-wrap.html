The story of this piece

The last of three wraps from this fragment. Together they give a sense of how a single broken piece can still hold several flattened “pages.”

What to look for

If you have looked at the other two, try to decide which rows feel most regular. Trust your doubt as much as your excitement.

What does the gold mean?

The ink on these scrolls is made of soot, just like the burnt papyrus, so it's nearly invisible in X-rays. An AI that learned from known letters paints gold wherever it thinks ink is. Brighter gold means it's more confident. It's a very good guess, not a reading.

For the curious: how this picture was made

This is one segment: a stretch of papyrus that the Vesuvius Challenge team traced through a 3D X-ray scan and flattened into a picture. The ink map is the team's published ink model, published by the team in the open data and used here with credit. We did not re-run the model. The scan view is the X-ray surface (the middle layers), sometimes shown at a slightly lower resolution so the site stays quick to load.

Size: 4,629 × 8,192 pixels (about 10 × 18 mm).
