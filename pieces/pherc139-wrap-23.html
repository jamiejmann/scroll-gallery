The story of this piece

This wrap has some of the densest marks in the published maps of this scroll. Remember that the body of On Gods is still being recovered: what you see is the AI's view of one flattened turn, not a finished page of the edition.

What to look for

Zoom in and follow a single row from left to right. Try “Hide faint marks” if the gold feels too busy.

What does the gold mean?

The ink on these scrolls is made of soot, just like the burnt papyrus, so it's nearly invisible in X-rays. An AI that learned from known letters paints gold wherever it thinks ink is. Brighter gold means it's more confident. It's a very good guess, not a reading.

For the curious: how this picture was made

This is one segment: a stretch of papyrus that the Vesuvius Challenge team traced through a 3D X-ray scan and flattened into a picture. The ink map is the team's published 1 µm ink model, published by the team in the open data and used here with credit. We did not re-run the model. The scan view is the X-ray surface (the middle layers), sometimes shown at a slightly lower resolution so the site stays quick to load.

Size: 7,538 × 8,192 pixels (about 9 × 9 mm).
