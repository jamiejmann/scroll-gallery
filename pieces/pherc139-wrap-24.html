The story of this piece

One turn on from the crowded wrap. The two sit close together inside the roll, so patterns can continue from one to the next — or break where the papyrus was crushed.

What to look for

Compare a busy patch here with Wrap 23. Do any rows seem to line up, the way writing would from one turn to the next?

What does the highlight mean?

The ink on these scrolls is made of soot, just like the burnt papyrus, so it's nearly invisible in X-rays. An AI that learned from known letters marks the spots where it thinks ink is. We show those marks in gold to start with. Under “More tools” you can switch to black, which looks like ink on paper, or red, which some people find easier to see. Stronger colour means the AI is more confident. It's a very good guess, not a reading.

For the curious: how this picture was made

This is one segment: a stretch of papyrus that the Vesuvius Challenge team traced through a 3D X-ray scan and flattened into a picture. The ink map is the team's published 1 µm ink model, published by the team in the open data and used here with credit. We did not re-run the model. The scan view is the X-ray surface (the middle layers), sometimes shown at a slightly lower resolution so the site stays quick to load.

Size: 7,648 × 8,192 pixels (about 9 × 9 mm).
