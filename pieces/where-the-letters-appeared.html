The story of this piece

In September 2026 the Vesuvius Challenge team reported finding letters inside this scroll, and the spot they pointed to is on this little piece of papyrus. Nobody has unrolled it: what you see was revealed with X-rays and AI, on an ordinary computer.

What to look for

Look at the upper right. A row of curved strokes and a round shape stand out from the rest. Do they look like letters to you? Experts make the final call, but this is the kind of mark they look for.

What does the gold mean?

The ink on these scrolls is made of soot, just like the burnt papyrus, so it's nearly invisible in X-rays. An AI that learned from known letters paints gold wherever it thinks ink is. Brighter gold means it's more confident. It's a very good guess, not a reading.

For the curious: how this picture was made

This is one segment: a stretch of papyrus that the Vesuvius Challenge team traced through a 3D X-ray scan (8.64 micrometres per pixel) and flattened into a picture. The ink map was made with Reader V3 by Domenico Russo, running on an ordinary processor (CPU) with the free reader at github.com/jamiejmann/vesuvius-cpu-reader. The scan view is the average of the five middle layers.

Size: 3,240 × 2,980 pixels (about 28 × 26 mm). Reading time: 1.9 minutes on 6 CPU threads. The team's picture is their own Reader V3 result, shared as a smaller preview (1/8 size).
